# Lessons the record teaches

Drawn from the repo's own files by an audit on 2026-10-07. The "my reading" items are
interpretation, not quotation.

1. **Symmetric absorption is the dominant failure.** If the incumbent may use your
   mechanism, you cannot separate from it by construction. Define a *class*, not a win
   against a baseline.
2. **Nearly every candidate is a known object in disguise.** Of 41 killed ideas, 19 had
   a mature home with all four winner properties and 0 were "nobody tried".
3. **A theorem is not a model.** The only substantive July result (ACDFT) is a lower
   bound for a narrow physical device class with "practical teeth zero".
4. **Absence of a proof is not a CANNOT.** Six failed proof routes are evidence about
   an archive, not an impossibility theorem.
5. **If the extension's data is derivable, it can be internal state.** Then any
   semantic separation is false and only a *priced* retention bound is honest. That
   usually reduces to streaming or INDEX.
6. **Exact translation kills.** Building a tiny CAN/CANNOT pair first is good (CIC,
   FrCC), but exact occupation by an existing field then kills it.
7. **Presentation and boundary choice are repeated killers.** Two constructors facing
   one phenomenon produced incompatible mathematics.
8. **Reviews found real errors.** A sign error, a violated clean-output contract, a
   Python digit limit, an empty proof buffer. The review process works at the level of
   correctness. (Caveat: reviewer and worker were the same Codex family.)
9. **Repair caps held.** One construction plus one repair converted every failure into
   a clean OPEN rather than endless patching.
10. **Apparatus outgrew artifact.** *(my reading)* Every phase accumulated scoped
    passes, controls and gates faster than it built the central object. The advisor
    raised the ratio at least three times. Later units (endurance wear, crit-bit nodes,
    serial-lane microstates) drifted from "a new model of computation" toward
    storage-coding results the advisor placed at "Floor to Middle-minus".
11. **Archive hygiene matters.** A proof spine was lost to a rewrite, a handoff kept
    saying READY after retraction, and the ledger kept incompatible "live" rows. Hash
    precommitment helped.
12. **Honest labels are strong here.** Withdrawals are recorded; source absence is
    never treated as novelty evidence.

13. **(2026-10-07/08) The same failure repeated with better tools.** Four more lines died to equal-resource translation (see ATTEMPTS.md Phase 5). A workspace-matched baseline,
    a retained cache, a sparse zero-tape simulation, and a standard cache policy each erased the apparent edge. Always build the strongest equal-access incumbent FIRST.
14. **Self-imposed restrictions on the incumbent manufacture advantages.** My own O(log)-workspace flat log made the construction look good until the permitted workspace was used.
15. **Check the checker.** An engine's confident citation list was 0 of 9 correct and its "experiment" was print statements; others were 17/17 and 9/9. Fetch every link; open and run the code.
16. **A finite gain is usually an omitted primitive.** AND "gained" at window 8 until an ordinary machine did it in three steps; one-gate savings vanished when the restoration NOT was charged.
17. **A clean kill is a result.** Prefer small experiments that can end an idea in an afternoon.
