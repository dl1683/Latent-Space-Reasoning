## Exact machines (T1, T2)

Fix finite `n`, public nonadaptive program, immutable input leaves `x_1,...,x_n`, borrowed Boolean wires `b_0,...,b_{k-1}` with **arbitrary** original values, and a writable one-bit output `y` promised initially **zero**. Every NOT, positive CNOT, or positive Toffoli is one invocation. Only `b_i,y` may be targets. The support of a CNOT is an edge of the undirected star centered at `b_0`; Toffoli's three-wire support must be connected in that star. Thus a Toffoli targeting the center may read two leaves, and one targeting an output or a noncenter borrower must use the center as a control. There are no resets, adaptive input-dependent gate choices, negative controls, copies of original borrower values, or free output/owner return.

At each *invocation boundary*, borrower `i` is **restored** iff its current bit equals its own original bit for **every** `x`, every original borrowed word, and every originally promised `y=0`. Its age is zero if restored and otherwise the previous age plus one. The peak of every age is at most `ell`; all borrowers are restored at the endpoint, and the endpoint output is `f(x)` independent of their original word. In particular, an accidental equality on some inputs is not a checkpoint. A *global* checkpoint restores every borrower; a one-borrower checkpoint is necessarily global. A word between consecutive global checkpoints need not restore borrowers on the **off-promise** initial-output-one states. For T1 the stronger optional full-target condition `(b,y)->(b,y+f(x))` will be identified separately; it is not assumed in the CANNOT.

## T1: one borrower, upper bound and short-lease classes

**Statement (PROVED-AT-SCOPE, code-assisted finite sublemma).** For `k=1`, every accepted `f` has Boolean ANF degree at most `ell+1`, at every total program length. At `ell=1` only constants occur. At `ell=2` and `ell=3` the class is *exactly* `RM_2(3,n)`, degree at most three (including constants). The latter classification uses the finite four-gate lemma independently checked below; it is **not** an inference from the general bound at `ell=3`.

**Proof of the checkpoint lemma.** For each fixed `x`, each gate is affine on two writable bits: with only two such bits, no gate can have both as controls. A word is therefore `(b',y')=(ab+cy+s,eb+dy+r)` over the Boolean ring, with `ad+ce=1` pointwise. At a restored checkpoint the current output is uniquely `y=P(x)b+Q(x)` (all functions of `(x,b)` have this form). The next restored checkpoint imposes `a+cP=1` and `cQ=s` pointwise, for **both** values of the original `b`. If `c=0`, invertibility and `a=1` force `d=1` and `s=0`. If `c=1`, `Q=s`. Thus in both cases `(1+d)(Q+s)=0`, and

`Q' + Q = (1+d)Q+r = (1+d)s+r =: D_word`.

The increment is independent of **both** the old `P` and `Q`, even though feasibility of the word is not. This is a pointwise identity, not a claim that *every* feasible graph is reachable. Starting from `Q=0`, telescope increments; at acceptance `P=0`, `Q=f`. On arbitrary initial output `y_0`, a checkpoint's graph has the extra `y_0` parameter and need not have the same `P,Q`; the theorem uses exactly the promised `y_0=0`. The explicit four-gate CAN below happens to satisfy the stronger full-target contract.

**Proof of the general bound.** If a segment starts restored and its first gate disturbs `b`, then after gates 1 through `ell` its possible dirty ages are 1 through `ell`; gate `ell+1` must close it. If the first gates leave `b` restored, they themselves are checkpoints. Hence each checkpoint word has `m<=ell+1` gates. Write gates as translations of one writable coordinate (`b+=p`, with `deg p<=2`, or `y+=1`) and shears (`b+=u y` or `y+=u b`, `u=1` or a single data variable). Matrix coefficients use only shears. Translation offsets are sums of transported **single** translation polynomials, so each term in `D=(1+d)s+r` uses at most one translated monomial and any subset of shear variables (overlaps only reduce support). With `t>=2` translations, degree <=`2+m-t<=m`; with no translations, `D=0`; with a single translation of degree <=1, degree <=`m`. For a single quadratic `b+=x_i x_j`, if any shear is uncontrolled, there are at most `m-2` distinct shear variables, so support <=`m`. Otherwise, if none of the `m-1` shear-control variables is `x_i` or `x_j`, specialize *all* those controls to zero and set `x_i=x_j=1`: only `b+=1` remains, and `b` cannot have been restored on **either** possible input output line. Contradiction. Thus at least one variable overlaps, again giving support <=`m`. This argument allows arbitrary precheckpoint `Q`, not just a clean output. Consequently `deg D<=m<=ell+1`; XOR of the increments preserves the bound.

**Short-lease lemma and finite proof.** Every <=4-gate word is represented, up to a renaming of its at most eight input names, by first-occurrence naming: a new singleton is the next unused name and a Toffoli with two new data controls names the next two in order. Enumerate these named gates, compose their scalar permutations on all `(x,b,y)` assignments, retaining the used-name count, and deduplicate identical `(permutation,used-name-count)` states. For each fixed `x` and `b`, demand at least one original output choice that returns `b`; this is exactly existence of some incoming graph `y=P(x)b+Q(x)`. Any two eligible output choices must have the same toggle, because two injective images inside the one-bit returned fibre either both fix or both swap. Compute the unique toggle on the original-`b=0` fibre for each `x`, then its Möbius/ANF degree. The independently written scalar `one_borrower()` produces successive new-map counts `8,63,597,7386` (total `8055`) and feasible degree histograms: gate 1 `{-1:4,0:1}`; gate 2 `{-1:22,0:6}`; gate 3 `{-1:111,0:36,1:12,2:21,3:6}`; gate 4 `{-1:599,0:188,1:146,2:391,3:198}`. There is **no** degree-four feasible increment at four gates; at <=2 gates only constant increments occur. The completeness argument is first-occurrence renaming plus exhaustive gate-generation and semantic deduplication, not a claim that the small finite receipt alone proves arbitrary length. For comparison, the earlier independent full labeled `n=8` search retained 1,978,827 maps; both enumerations agree on the maximum degrees.

At lease one, two-gate segments have constant increments; at leases two and three, segments are respectively <=3 and <=4 gates and have degree<=3. Conversely, for any monomial `p q` with `deg p<=2`, `deg q<=1`, `p` a nonempty positive monomial, use

`b+=p; y+=q b; b+=p; y+=q b`.

`q=1` handles degrees one and two. It restores `b` after gate 3 (peak age two), toggles `pq` for both original `b` and **either** original `y`, and incurs all four invocations. XOR these words and use `NOT y` for the constant. This gives the exact classes. **So what:** the deadline bounds ANF degree at arbitrary work length, but the identical NCT circuit on an ordinary machine is an equal-resource simulation.

## T2: any finite number of borrowers at lease two

**Statement (PROVED-AT-SCOPE).** For every fixed finite `k>=1` on the **single** star and the promised `y=0` contract, the only accepted functions at `ell=1` are constants; the accepted functions at `ell=2` are exactly `RM_2(3,n)` at any program length. Do not transfer this to `R_double`, mutable data, or negative controls. The global-checkpoint increment lemma below applies to any finite `k`, not just the star.

**Proof of increment independence.** Fix `x` and an original borrower word `v`. A reversible word permutes all `(borrowed,y)` states. Within the two-element input fibre `{(v,0),(v,1)}`, a valid returned input output is one whose image has borrowed word `v`. If both are valid, their distinct images fill that same two-element output fibre, hence their toggles (image output XOR input output) coincide. If only one is valid, its toggle is fixed anyway. When a whole incoming checkpoint graph is returned, this uniquely defines `D_word(x,v)`, independent of the previous output graph. In particular fixing `v=0^k`, successive global increments XOR to `f`; no assumption about restoring arbitrary original output was inserted.

**Proof of the schedule lemma.** Partition the execution into maximal intervals containing a dirty borrower, beginning/ending at globally restored boundaries. At lease two, the opening gate has one borrower as target (one gate cannot open two borrowers). If gate 2 opens another borrower, gate 3 is forced to close the first (its age would otherwise be three), and gate 4 must close the second. All four targets are borrowed, so this overlap component contains no output gate. If gate 2 does not open another, then gate 3 must close the first; any output change in this component uses at most three gates and only that one dirty borrower. An apparent earlier restoration simply splits the component; a new borrower cannot open on a gate that closes the old one. Outside components every borrower is at its original value.

Fix the original borrowed vector to zero. Outside components, all data-controlled output gates have `b_0` as a positive control and vanish; only `NOT y` survives. Inside a one-borrower <=3-gate component, if the dirty borrower is not the center, the still-zero center disables **every** data-dependent output gate (an output CNOT from the noncenter borrower is disconnected). If the center is the dirty borrower, every other borrower remains zero on this slice and its controlled gates vanish. The surviving effective actions form a <=3-gate one-borrower star word with a feasible returned input line, so T1's <=3-gate increment degree<=3. The global fibre lemma justifies telescoping despite earlier output dirt. For lease one, each dirty component must close in its second gate and therefore has no output gate; only output NOT gates survive on the zero slice. The four-gate T1 compiler with `b=b_0` gives all cubic functions, leaving every extra borrower untouched. **So what:** adding borrowers does not change the lease-two class under this particular star contract.

Those 4-gate checks **do not** cover longer programs; the schedule proof does.

## T3: finite-group output, one-way gate alphabet, independent reconstruction

**Exact model.** Different from T1/T2: one arbitrary borrowed bit, immutable Boolean `x`, output `z` in a **fixed finite group** `G`, required `(b,z)->(b,F(x)z)` for **every** initial `b,z`. One gate each: `A_p` toggles `b` by a positive monomial `p` of degree <=2 (including 1); `O_g` left-multiplies output by `g`; `B_(q,g)` left-multiplies by `g` when `b q(x)=1`, with `q` a monomial of degree <=1 (including 1). No output-to-borrower control. Restoration on both initial-borrower values has peak age <=2. Every group element is an available label, not a free program. This is the earlier r12 model, **not** NCT and not an arbitrary group branching program.

**Upper bound from scratch (VERIFIED, all finite groups at lease two).** Let `A=G/[G,G]`, written additively, and let `t_j` be the image of each `B` label. On the `b_initial=0` slice, at any `B` gate the current borrower is either restored (`a_j=0`), or the immediately preceding gate was a single `A_p` (`a_j=p`), since a dirty borrower may remain dirty for at most two boundaries. A `B` cannot appear immediately after two unreturned `A` gates, and an `O` between an opening `A` and a `B` would itself consume the second dirty boundary. Thus `a_j` is either zero or a *single positive monomial* of degree <=2. The abelianized output is

`bar F_0(x)=C+sum_j t_j [a_j(x) q_j(x)]`,

where brackets are Boolean indicators and `C` is the sum of unconditional output labels. At lease two each `a_j q_j` is a positive monomial of degree <=3, so `bar F` has no Boolean-lattice Möbius coefficients above degree three in **any** abelian group `A`. For `b_initial=1`, replace every `a_j` with `1-a_j` (Boolean complement). Equality of both borrower slices gives, by adding their abelian output values,

`2 bar F(x) = 2C + sum_j t_j q_j(x)`.

Consequently `2 bar F` is affine (this part needs **no** lease). For exponent-two `A`, this second constraint is vacuous; for odd-order `A`, multiplication by 2 is invertible and `bar F` is affine even without a lease. This proves the C2/S3/S4 degree-three sign bounds and the A4 `A4/V4=C3` affine bound. A5 has trivial abelianization; this upper bound says nothing there. The additive ANF over general `A` uses integer Möbius inversion, **not** an unjustified mod-two XOR of group labels. **Failed attack against the overbroad intermediate claim:** without the lease, `a=x1x2 XOR x3x4` is a quadratic *binary* ANF but its integer indicator is `x1x2+x3x4-2x1x2x3x4`, not an integer polynomial of degree two. The lease-two short excursion, not just the prefix-degree bound, is needed for the general quotient-degree conclusion.

**Matching construction for the five named groups (REPAIRED at named scope, not all groups; self-rederivation, not independent review of T3).** A four-gate word `A_1; B_(x_i,g); A_1; B_(x_i,g)` yields `g^(x_i)` even for non-involutive `g`, since the two `B` gates fire on complementary original-borrower values. For involutive `h`, `A_p;B_(q,h);A_p;B_(q,h)` yields `h^(pq)` with total degree <=3. A constant uses `O_g`. All these words have peak two, return the borrower and act correctly for every initial group output. If `H(x)=h` on predicate `U` and identity otherwise, and `K(x)=g` on predicate `V` and identity otherwise, the word `H K H^-1 K^-1` is localized to `U AND V`, with element a conjugate/order-convention version of `[h,g]`; inverse words reverse order and invert labels, so also have peak two. Start with `O_h`, then inductively commutate with one-literal `g^(x_i)`; replace `x_i` by `1-x_i` using `O_g` followed by `(g^(x_i))^-1`. This constructs a single nontrivial element at any minterm if the iterated commutator remains nontrivial. Conjugating by constant words and multiplying same-minterm words realizes its whole normal closure, independently at each minterm.

For the four nonabelian groups, explicit permutations `(images of 0,1,...)` for `(h,g)` whose iterated commutators cycle through nonidentity elements with the required normal closures are: `S3 ((1,2,0),(0,2,1))`, `A4 ((1,0,3,2),(0,2,3,1))`, `S4 ((0,2,3,1),(0,1,3,2))`, `A5 ((0,1,3,4,2),(0,2,3,1,4))`. The finite multiplication/normal-closure calculation in `r13_probe.py` yields respectively closure orders `3,4,12,60` for every phase, and scalar minterm replays for `n=1..6` yield nonidentity at exactly `1^n` on **both** borrowed slices. These are finite group certificates: checking closure takes at most 60 group elements and the displayed pair cycles, so the induction extends to arbitrary `n`. For C2, constants and involutive cubic-monomial echoes synthesize exactly `RM_2(3,n)`. In S3 and S4, sign-lift an arbitrary cubic Boolean polynomial by the transposition echo and fix the residual S3's A3 / S4's A4 pointwise by minterms. In A4, lift any affine C3 table using constants and one-literal controls, then fix V4 pointwise. In A5, its perfect quotient is trivial and the normal closure is the whole group. All synthesis is finite but can be exponential.

**Necessary qualification, and a counterexample to the *general* matching claim.** The phrase "constrained **only** on `G/[G,G]`" is **false for unrestricted finite G**. Let `G=D8=<r,s | r^4=s^2=1, srs=r^-1>`, `c=r^2`, so `[G,G]=<c>` and `G/[G,G]=C2^2`. Write group elements uniquely as `r^u s^v c^w`, `u,v,w in F2`. The product sends `u,v` to XOR sums and sends `w` to `w+w'+u u'+v u'` (up to the harmless convention for multiplication order). On initial `b=0`, every individual `B` firing predicate has Boolean degree <=3 and every `O` has constant predicate. Express each `g` in these coordinates and multiply the factors: the cumulative `u,v` have degree <=3 and the cumulative `w` degree <=6, **at arbitrary word length and even without a lease**. Therefore the table `F(x)=c^(x_1...x_7)` is impossible, despite its completely trivial abelian projection. This refutes a universal matching construction for all finite groups; nontrivial commutator subgroup is not enough. For abelian `G` the exact statement *can* be sharpened: `F` has Möbius degree <=3 and each degree-2 or degree-3 coefficient lies in `G[2]`; necessity follows from `2F` affine, sufficiency from one-literal arbitrary labels and involutive echoes. An arbitrary nonabelian-group quotient lift may fail even when its quotient meets these necessary conditions; no general sufficiency theorem was proved here.
