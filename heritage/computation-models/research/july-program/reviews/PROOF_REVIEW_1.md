## 1. ACDFT lemma

### 1.1 Frozen review scope

The packet asks for independent verification of: (i) the stable cross-cut dimension, (ii) the finite-grid projective packing bound `(P)`, (iii) the failure-aware interactive cut-information lower bound, and (iv) only a conditional geometry assembly - not an unconditional area-time theorem (`ACDFT:L450-L469`). The mandatory attacks include cut placement, cube sections, rounding, phase-orbit counting, remote decoding, feedback/shared randomness, hard-codebook advice, and exact-rank occupation (`ACDFT:L471-L494`).

### 1.2 Findings

1. **SOUND — the quotient distance and frozen grid are well formed (`ACDFT:L17-L60`; `MISSION:L232-L270`).** The displayed `d_coh` is the orbit pseudometric for the isometric action of `U(1)` on complex Euclidean space and therefore induces a genuine metric on global-phase orbits, including the zero orbit. For `b>=1`, `Q_b>0`; each real and imaginary coordinate has endpoints `+-1/sqrt(2N)`, and the direct norm calculation gives `||x||_2<=1`. Nothing in the lemma silently chooses a per-coordinate phase or changes the registered endpoint convention.

2. **SOUND — the robust cut count has the correct direction, dimension, and constants (`ACDFT:L17-L37`, `ACDFT:L77-L112`).** The direct sum has total complex domain and codomain dimension `N`, even for an extreme input placement. Every entry contributes `1/N`, so `||B||_F^2=N/2`; coordinate compression of a unitary has operator norm at most one. If `k` of the padded `N` singular values are at least `1/2`, then `N/2=sum sigma_j^2 <= k+(N-k)/4`, hence `k>=N/3`. The singular multiset of a direct sum is the union of the two blocks' singular multisets, so one direction contributes at least `N/6`. This also survives the boundary placement `I_L=emptyset` or `I_R=emptyset`.

3. **SOUND — Vaaler's theorem is imported with the correct normalization (`ACDFT:L123-L184`).** The primary source states that every `K`-dimensional linear section of the volume-one cube `[-1/2,1/2]^M` has induced `K`-volume at least one (J. D. Vaaler, 1979, p. 543 and the corollary on p. 544). Scaling that cube by `2a` gives exactly `vol_d(E intersect [-a,a]^M)>=(2a)^d`; no ambient-dimension factor appears. Realifying a `k`-dimensional complex subspace gives `d=2k` with the same Euclidean norm.

4. **SOUND — the Euclidean packing count does not lose a dimension (`ACDFT:L186-L203`).** A maximal `tau`-separated set covers the compact section by radius-`tau` balls in its intrinsic `d`-space, giving `|P| >= (2a)^d/(v_d tau^d)`. Here `d=2k`, so `v_d=pi^k/k!` and `k!>=(k/e)^k` directly imply `v_d^(1/d)<=sqrt(2 pi e/d)`. Substituting `a=1/sqrt(2N)`, `tau=24 rho`, and `d/N>=1/3` yields precisely `(24 sqrt(3 pi e) rho)^(-d)=(c_V/rho)^d`.

5. **SOUND — rounding lands in the unchanged codebook and the `sqrt(N)` factor is present (`ACDFT:L205-L242`).** The scalar spacing is `h=a/Q_b`; at most `2N` real coordinates move by `h/2`, giving total error `1/(2Q_b)<=2^{-b}`. The endpoints are grid points, zero-padding is legal, and all rounded vectors lie in `X_{N,b}`. For two continuous witnesses, stable gain and two perturbations give `12 rho-2*2^{-b} >= 10 rho`; thus rounding neither collides inputs nor outputs. The norm bound `||A w_tilde||<=1` follows from the coordinate cube and `||A||_op<=1`.

6. **SOUND — phase-orbit pruning and its logarithmic correction are valid (`ACDFT:L244-L271`).** A radius-at-most-one phase orbit has arclength at most `2 pi` and is coverable by at most `1+16 pi/s` balls of radius `s/8`. Any neighbor in the graph lies within `3s/8` of a selected orbit center; two neighbors assigned to one center would be at Euclidean distance below `3s/4<s`, contradicting the definition of `s`. Hence the stated degree bound is safe (indeed slightly loose), and the still looser greedy denominator `3+8 pi/(5 rho)` is valid. Independence gives projective separation at least `s/4 >= (5/2)rho >2 epsilon`. The positive-part/singleton case is handled consistently.

7. **SOUND — the rate-distortion/codebook step genuinely replaces exact recovery (`ACDFT:L273-L277`, `ACDFT:L330-L368`).** The proof first constructs exponentially many finite grid inputs whose remote ideal outputs are separated in the registered quotient metric; it never assumes that a Fourier subblock is injective on all remote inputs. Thompson's source proof instead lower-bounds exact communication by injectivity/entropy of exact finite-ring outputs (extracted Thompson thesis, `L1297-L1319`). Blake–Kschischang's result likewise concerns exact recovery of fair source bits for BEC/BSC coding circuits (extracted Blake–Kschischang, `L571-L578`, `L713-L719`, `L2021-L2023`). Thus exactness is not sneaking into the new decoding argument: only nearest packing-point identification on the success event is exact, which is exactly the standard reduction from distortion to finite-message recovery.

8. **SOUND — restriction, Fano, shared randomness, and the directional capacity comparison are valid (`ACDFT:L283-L379`; `GATE0:L80-L101`).** With the opposite-side input fixed to zero, restricting a globally successful output to `O_R` cannot increase the coherent error because the same execution-dependent phase remains admissible. Nearest-projective decoding errs with probability at most `delta`. Since the hard index is uniform and independent of input-independent `R_0`, conditional Fano gives `I(J;V_R|R_0,X_R=0)>=(1-delta)log_2|C|-h_2(delta)`. Conditional data processing is applicable, and the capacity certificate quantifies over every finite input law and the complete interactive view, so feedback, channel noise, local randomness, and coding are not double-counted. Xu–Raginsky independently supports the generic cutset template for interactive finite-capacity networks (extracted Xu–Raginsky, `L185-L193`, `L444-L501`), while Lee–Shraibman is confined to sign matrices/Boolean communication (extracted Lee–Shraibman, `L21-L25`, `L415-L449`).

9. **SOUND — cut-dependent subcode selection is legitimate and is not advice (`ACDFT:L157-L160`, `ACDFT:L332-L336`, `ACDFT:L413-L416`).** The architecture is fixed first and must satisfy the success promise for every member of the cut-independent ambient grid. A worst-case proof may then choose a distribution on that already-required input set separately for each cut. The selected subcode is used only by the analyst's decoder and is never supplied to the architecture; arbitrary-real singular vectors remain proof witnesses, not machine state.

10. **MAJOR — the conditional area–time display squares a possibly negative lower bound (`ACDFT:L425-L448`).** Let
   `Q=(1-delta)[(N/3)log_2(c_V/rho)-Gamma(rho)]^+-h_2(delta)`.
   The premises imply `T kappa sqrt(A) >= Q`, but when `Q<0` they do not imply `A T^2 >= Q^2/kappa^2`. This fails, for example, whenever the packing bracket is zero and `0<delta<1/2`: then `Q=-h_2(delta)<0`, while the displayed right-hand side is positive. The exact statement-preserving repair is to replace the parenthesis before squaring by `[Q]^+=max{Q,0}`, or explicitly restrict the display to `Q>=0`. This does not damage `(RCUT)`, but it makes the artifact as written mathematically false in an allowed boundary regime and makes the claim that all correction terms are displayed (`ACDFT:L447-L448`) too strong.

11. **SOUND, subject to Finding 10 — the core asymptotic scope is honest (`ACDFT:L307-L328`, `ACDFT:L381-L424`).** Under `epsilon<c_V/4` and the stated fixed `b`, `rho<c_V/2`, so the packing coefficient is positive and the `O(log(1/rho))` phase penalty is lower order in `N`; uniform `delta<delta_0<1/2` gives `Omega(N)`. The artifact correctly withholds an unconditional planar geometry law, physical channel certification, a novelty-final claim, optical advantage, quotient-task inheritance, and the full ACDFT-AT theorem. Its open-problem list is incomplete only in failing to list the sign/squaring condition found above.

### 1.3 Break attempts

1. **Successful boundary counterexample — conditional square.** Take any `N,b`, set `epsilon=1` and advertise any allowed `delta` with `0<delta<1/2` (for example `delta=1/4`). Because every registered input has norm at most one and `F_N` is unitary, an architecture whose named output terminals are initialized to zero succeeds for every input at time `T=0`: `d_coh(0,F_N x)=||x||<=1`. It may have zero cut capacity. The bracket in `Q` is zero, so `(RCUT)` says only `0>=-h_2(delta)`, which is true. For any finite `kappa>0`, the hypothesized geometry inequality `C_cut<=kappa sqrt(A)` is also true. But `ACDFT:L435-L445` would assert `0=A T^2>=h_2(delta)^2/kappa^2>0`. This breaks the displayed conditional assembly and confirms Finding 10.

2. **Failed break — extreme and finite-size cuts.** I put every input terminal on one physical side, making one cross block empty, and also exhaustively checked every output bisection and every input placement for `N=2,4,8`. The minimum observed total number of singular values at least `1/2` was respectively `1,2,4`, versus required `N/3`; the better directional block had respectively `1,1,2`, versus required `N/6`. The analytic Frobenius/operator-norm proof explains why no placement can do worse (`ACDFT:L90-L112`).

3. **Failed break — degenerate grid and threshold.** At `b=1` or `rho>=c_V`, the quantitative lower bound becomes zero and the singleton branch is valid; no nonexistent exponential packing is claimed. As `rho` approaches the nontrivial threshold from below, positivity is demanded only after subtracting `Gamma(rho)`, and if it is positive then `(c_V/rho)^d>2^Gamma` guarantees at least two pre-pruning points. Coordinate endpoints remain legal and the two-error rounding margin still leaves `10 rho` separation (`ACDFT:L202-L242`).

4. **Failed break — phase-collinear family.** I tried to place many Euclidean-separated outputs on one phase orbit, which would all represent one projective point. Such a circle can contain only `O(1/s)` points at Euclidean separation `s`; the orbit-cover injection in `(6)` charges exactly this loss. After the independent-set pruning, no two retained points can be phase-equivalent (`ACDFT:L244-L271`).

5. **Failed break — execution-dependent global phase and feedback.** A successful full output may choose a different optimizing phase on every execution, but restricting coordinates preserves that same phase and hence the success event. The decoder needs only the final remote terminal vector; conditioning on input-independent shared randomness makes Fano compatible with arbitrary interactive feedback already covered by the operational capacity certificate (`ACDFT:L338-L379`; `GATE0:L80-L101`).

6. **Failed break — arbitrary-real hard advice.** The packing subspace and its witnesses can depend on the fixed architecture and cut, but only rounded members of the pre-existing finite grid are used as inputs. Since the machine must succeed on every grid point, choosing a hard distribution after fixing the machine supplies no advice to it (`ACDFT:L157-L160`, `ACDFT:L332-L336`).

### 1.4 Verdict

**REPAIR.** Candidate Lemma 1, Candidate Lemma 2, and the information statement `(RCUT)` are mathematically upheld at their exact stated scopes. The artifact as a whole is not upheld because the conditional area-time assembly at `ACDFT:L425-L448` is false for allowed parameters. Exact repair list:

1. In the area-time display, define `Q` as in Finding 10 and replace `Q^2/kappa^2` by `([Q]^+)^2/kappa^2`; equivalently state the squared formula only under the added condition `Q>=0`.
2. Add this nonnegative-part/sufficiently-large-`N` condition to the `Still broken or open` scope list and stop claiming that the present display contains all correction terms.
3. Keep `CANDIDATE DERIVATION - UNREVIEWED`; under `ACDFT:L485-L494`, the repaired packet requires a new fresh reviewer before any reviewed/`PROVED` token. No task, metric, codebook, phase quotient, or architecture change is needed.

## 2. BLSD-D

### 2.1 Frozen review scope

The standalone packet freezes a uniformly generated bounded-degree graph, finite encoded state, local finite kernels, complete physical footprints, finite randomized sampler circuits, full-footprint conflict coloring, input-uniform conditional TV errors, same-graph preparation/readout, and one finite constructor (`BLSD:L422-L497`). The theorem claims only the unrolled circuit bounds `(D3)`-`(D6)` and constructor-uniform greedy coloring; it separately fixes the novelty status as `OCCUPIED-CORE / UNESTABLISHED WRAPPER` (`BLSD:L499-L543`, `BLSD:L631-L644`). The mandatory attacks are exactly hazards/fanout, conditional products, TV telescoping, resource omissions, uniform schedule construction, and model drift (`BLSD:L646-L669`).

### 2.2 Findings

1. **SOUND — the same-color semantics exclude every stated overlap hazard by hypothesis (`BLSD:L439-L474`).** If two blocks have the same color then their complete footprints are disjoint. Because each footprint contains reads, writes, local routes, copies, random-bit inputs, decoding, and commit buffers, this implies disjoint read sets, write sets, circuits, and routes. The scope is deliberately stronger than ordinary data-hazard coloring because it serializes read/read overlap as well. This is restrictive but internally consistent, and it blocks free shared-state fanout rather than assuming it.

2. **SOUND — `(D8)` remains correct under a random, correlated pre-sublayer state (`BLSD:L452-L474`, `BLSD:L557-L592`).** Conditional on the complete state `x`, each sampler sees a fixed local input and uses fresh bits independent of every other same-color sampler. Disjoint writes make the conditional output law the tensor product shown in `(D8)`, with untouched state coordinates deterministically retained. Unconditionally, the outputs may be correlated through `x`; the proof never claims otherwise and integrates a uniform conditional bound afterward.

3. **SOUND — the parallel-product TV sum `(D9)` has no missing independence or measurability premise (`BLSD:L452-L460`, `BLSD:L557-L586`).** All spaces are finite. Sequentially replacing one tensor factor gives `TV(tensor P_u,tensor Q_u)<=sum_u TV(P_u,Q_u)`, and `(D1)` supplies the bound uniformly for every encoded input. The stated fresh-bit independence is precisely what is needed to identify the implemented conditional kernel as a product.

4. **SOUND — the full telescope `(D10)` and error total `(D6)` are correct (`BLSD:L594-L609`).** Triangle inequality plus Markov-kernel contraction gives
   `TV(nu K,nu_hat K_hat)<=TV(nu,nu_hat)+sup_x TV(K(x),K_hat(x))`.
   Iterating from preparation through every ordered sublayer and then through ideal/implemented readout yields exactly `eta_P+sum eta_u+eta_R`. The upper bound may exceed one, but that only makes it loose, not false.

5. **SOUND — depth, gate-size, and random-bit bookkeeping match the construction (`BLSD:L501-L517`, `BLSD:L588-L618`).** Pairwise footprint-disjoint samplers in a color run concurrently, so their depth is the maximum and their gates/random bits add; color sublayers and logical layers run sequentially, so depths add. Every sampler circuit occurs once in the unrolling, and preparation/readout occur once. Commit operations, local copies, routes, finite decoding, and random-bit inputs are charged inside the declared sampler resources.

6. **SOUND — the peak-state bound is valid under the packet's declared meaning of `a_u` (`BLSD:L445-L450`, `BLSD:L519-L525`, `BLSD:L611-L618`).** The persistent encoded configuration costs `qn`. Only one preparation, readout, or color sublayer is live at a time; within a color, ancillary and output-buffer storage adds, while between colors it can be released. Thus `qn+max{a_P,a_R,max_{t,c}sum_u a_u}` is a safe upper bound. This certification relies on the explicit hypothesis that each `a_u` already includes every live intermediate/commit buffer; it is not a derived bound from `d_u,z_u,rho_u` alone.

7. **SOUND — schedule generation is uniform and is not hidden execution advice (`BLSD:L494-L497`, `BLSD:L537-L543`, `BLSD:L620-L626`).** Finite footprint descriptions make intersection decidable; deterministic greedy coloring in a constructor-fixed order uses at most `D_t^conf+1` colors. The packet expressly declines to bound offline constructor time or schedule-description complexity by `(D3)`-`(D5)`. That is a conventional uniform-circuit-family scope, not a missing runtime charge.

8. **SOUND — preparation, readout, routing, and terminal materialization are within the frozen boundary (`BLSD:L476-L492`, `BLSD:L528-L534`, `BLSD:L606-L618`).** They are supplied same-graph local circuits with their own resource/error tuples, and the theorem does not manufacture a central state, remote pointer, global route, or second materialization. The result is an unrolling theorem for already-supplied finite local samplers, not a compiler from an abstract kernel description to an efficient sampler.

9. **SOUND — the novelty label does not exceed the evidence (`BLSD:L631-L644`).** Primary sources do establish local/distributed Gibbs sampling and parallel local Glauber updates: Feng–Sun–Yin give LOCAL-model Markov-chain samplers including random-independent-set parallel updates, Feng–Yin relate local sampling/inference/counting, and Fischer–Ghaffari give an `O(log n)`-round local Glauber chain in their Dobrushin regime. None of this source evidence is used to claim the exact ledger wrapper is novel. Calling scheduled local-kernel composition an occupied elementary core and the wrapper `UNESTABLISHED` is conservative.

### 2.3 Break attempts

1. **Failed break — single-site device.** For `n=1` and one local update, the conflict graph has one vertex and one color. `(D3)`-`(D6)` reduce to direct serial composition of preparation, that sampler, and readout. For no updates, the sublayer sum is empty and only preparation/readout remain. No bound assumes `n>1`, a nontrivial cut, or parallelism (`BLSD:L432-L446`, `BLSD:L499-L535`).

2. **Failed break — two blocks sharing only a read or a route.** Even if their logical write sets are disjoint, a shared read vertex, wire, copy gate, or route lies in both complete footprints. The conflict graph therefore gives the blocks different colors, converting the supposed parallel counterexample into two sequential sublayers and making both depth and state accounting valid (`BLSD:L448-L474`).

3. **Failed break — common random pre-state.** Let two disjoint samplers read components that are perfectly correlated under the incoming law. Their outputs can remain correlated unconditionally, so a naive unconditional product proof would fail. Here `(D8)` is asserted only after conditioning on the complete state; `(D9)` is uniform in that state and is then integrated. The telescope therefore survives this strongest probabilistic attack (`BLSD:L557-L586`).

4. **Failed break — error accumulation through adversarial intervening laws.** I replaced each implemented sublayer one at a time while allowing all earlier errors to change the current state distribution. `(D10)` uses a supremum over states, so no step needs the implemented and ideal processes to have the same incoming law. Markov contraction prevents prior TV error from amplifying (`BLSD:L594-L609`).

5. **Failed break — huge or cut-specific schedule.** Greedy coloring may take large offline time and the schedule may be large, but the theorem claims neither a constructor-time bound nor description size in `(D3)`-`(D5)`. It requires one finite uniform constructor and includes the resulting schedule in the generated circuit description, so there is no nonuniform advice claim to falsify (`BLSD:L537-L543`, `BLSD:L620-L626`).

### 2.4 Verdict

**UPHELD.** BLSD-D may be labeled `PROVED` only at this certified scope: uniform composition of the already-supplied finite same-graph preparation, fully footprint-scheduled local randomized sampler circuits, and readout, with the exact bounds `(D3)`-`(D6)` and the declared resource meanings in section 5.1. The eligible repository token is **`BLSD-D-PROVED-AFTER-REVIEW / OCCUPIED-CORE`** (`MISSION:L480-L489`; `BLSD:L664-L668`). This does **not** certify novelty of the wrapper, efficient construction of local samplers from ideal kernels, BLSD-CT, a continuous-time compiler, a new model of computation, or a mission-level result.
