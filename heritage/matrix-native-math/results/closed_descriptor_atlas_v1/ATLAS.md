## Theorem pair (revised again 2026-10-03 after a cold no-memory referee; the referee verdicts are recorded here)
THEOREM C-P (TRIVIAL AND KNOWN; referee: CORRECT-WITH-REPAIR). [Correction from round-2 review (Codex 6.1): add the hypothesis that F is irreducible, e.g. the image of an irreducible variety; F = {0, I} is product-closed and gives a 0-dimensional but reducible closure, so 'irreducible semigroup' fails without it; and {rank <= r} for r < n is a semigroup WITHOUT identity, not a monoid.] Let F be a constructible subset of M_n(C) (e.g. the image of a regular or rational map from an irreducible m-dimensional variety) with F F contained in the Zariski closure of F. Then S = closure(F) is an irreducible algebraic subsemigroup of M_n and dim S <= m. Proof (repaired, standard; Putcha 1988, Renner 2005, Humphreys 7.4): for a in F, L_a is a morphism, so L_a^{-1}(S) is closed and contains F, hence contains S, so F S is contained in S; for s in S, R_s^{-1}(S) is closed and contains F by the previous step, hence contains S, so S S is contained in S. dim S <= m because the closure of the image of an irreducible m-dimensional variety has dimension <= m. Caveats: algebraically closed field (over F_q the statement is vacuous; the meaningful version is F_q-points of a variety defined over F_q); closure under product of F itself is not needed, only F F inside closure(F); the theorem is a tautology about Zariski closures and says NOTHING about computational cost.
COUNTEREXAMPLE to 'low dimension' for non-regular parametrisations (referee): phi(t) = diag(e^(lambda_1 t), ..., e^(lambda_n t)) with lambda_i Q-linearly independent is a one-parameter product-closed family whose Zariski closure is the full n-dimensional torus. The semigroup conclusion survives, the dimension bound does not.
CORRECTION to (N): 'rank-defined varieties are not semigroups of small dimension' is WRONG as written - {rank <= r} is itself a closed algebraic monoid of dimension r(2n-r) with O(n r) action (row 0). The honest statement: displacement rank, HSS/H-matrix off-diagonal rank and Monarch structure are NOT closed under product because ranks ADD (row 7-9); whether the Zariski closure of the generated semigroup is all of M_n for each of these is plausible, partly checked (dim Mon^2 grows), NOT proved. The earlier sentence that the growth-forcing classes 'are exactly families not regularly parametrised by few coordinates closed under product' was circular and is withdrawn. Putcha/Renner relevance is limited to the part where the closure is a reductive monoid (Kronecker, symmetric powers, Gaussian lifts); general closures can be non-reductive (unipotent, triangular).
CATALOGUE STATEMENT C-N' (referee: a restatement, not a conjecture): the mission-relevant content is the list of pairs (algebraic monoid S, representation / parametrisation) together with (i) the cost of APPLYING an element to a vector, (ii) inverse and conversion costs, (iii) native supply of coordinates in a workload. Known entries: low rank O(n r); Kronecker O(d p n); binary forms O(d log d) (measured linear in d); exterior/Gaussian O(d^3) per amplitude; group FFTs (Maslen-Rockmore, Clausen). Every one of these is a KNOWN algorithm, so the only claims the project can make about them are (a) the unifying indexing, (b) priced comparison against the dense status quo and against handed-the-matrix methods, and (c) exclusion statements for non-closed families once proved.
Consequence (HEURISTIC): the seven controls and the atlas entries show a single mechanism: closed bounded families are algebraic monoids, whose fast actions are known; the project's open mathematical question is NOT existence of such families but (1) the exclusion theorems for rank-defined formats (is the generated semigroup dense in M_n for r >= 2 displacement rank? what is the exact growth law in Zariski dimension?), and (2) whether any monoid/representation pair outside the known fast-action list gives a new o(n^2) action.

## What would change this atlas (decisive tests)
1. A pair (S, rho) with a PROVED o(n^2) action algorithm not in the known list, with a workload supplying coordinates natively, priced against the dense status quo and a coordinate-aware specialist (Amendment 1 comparison rule).
2. A proof or counterexample that the semigroup generated by displacement-rank-r matrices (or Monarch matrices) has Zariski closure M_n, with the exact growth of dim of the k-fold product variety (a Floor-tier exclusion theorem).
3. Whether non-algebraic (analytic) bounded families have any computational use beyond their algebraic closure.
