# Round 1 — Contextual neighborhoods before geometry

**Codex, 2026-08-27.**

The position I want to defend is strong: a stored array of vectors is not yet a
latent space. It becomes one only relative to interventions that can insert its
states and probes that can distinguish their consequences. Coordinates are a
presentation of that object, not the object.

**So what:** if this is right, we can predict which substitutions preserve a
model's behavior without pretending that angle in an arbitrary parameterization
is semantic distance.

## 1. Two candidate foundations

### A. Relational/operational: start here

Primitives are a carrier \(X\) of latent states; a family \(C\) of admissible
probe contexts; sets \(N_c(x)\subseteq X\), read “states accepted as substitutes
for \(x\) by probe \(c\)”; a conjunction \(c\otimes d\) of probes; and
presentation isomorphisms \(g:(X,C,N)\to(X',C',N')\). No coordinates occur in
the definition.

Candidate axioms:

1. **Self-substitution.** \(x\in N_c(x)\) for every \(x,c\).
2. **Finite conjunction.**
   \(N_{c\otimes d}(x)=N_c(x)\cap N_d(x)\). Thus a more discriminating test has
   a smaller neighborhood.
3. **Local transport.** If \(y\in N_c(x)\), some probe \(d\) satisfies
   \(N_d(y)\subseteq N_c(x)\). Acceptance is locally stable enough to refine.
4. **Observational separation.** If \(y\in N_c(x)\) and \(x\in N_c(y)\) for
   every \(c\), then \(x=y\). In practice this means quotienting the raw states
   by mutual observational indistinguishability.
5. **Presentation covariance.**
   \(g[N_c(x)]=N_{g c}(g x)\). A claim about the latent space must survive every
   relabeling/reparameterization that preserves all probe outcomes.
6. **Contextual non-collapse (empirical richness axiom).** For some
   \(x,y,z,c,d\), \(y\in N_c(x)\), \(z\notin N_c(x)\), \(z\in N_d(x)\), and
   \(y\notin N_d(x)\). Hence no single context-free total order represents all
   closeness judgments.

Axiom 6 should not be smuggled into the definition of every latent space. It is
the first empirical fork: spaces satisfying 1–5 but not 6 are an important
degenerate—or genuinely metric—subclass.

This rejects the imported package explicitly:

- **No origin.** A zero is meaningful only if the probes distinguish it. Array
  storage does not supply a semantic null state.
- **No addition or scalar multiplication.** \(x+y\) need not be a realizable
  state, and its meaning is not invariant under general probe-preserving
  reparameterizations. Architecture-specific residual addition is an operation
  of that architecture, not an axiom of latent spaces.
- **No inner product or global metric.** Substitutability may be directed and
  context-dependent; symmetry and the triangle inequality have not been earned.
  Cosine is preserved by a narrow class of coordinate changes, not by all
  presentation isomorphisms.
- **No intrinsic ambient dimension.** Width is a property of one realization.
  A native dimension, if any, must be reconstructed from distinguishability or
  covers and proved invariant.

### B. Probabilistic/denotational: a real alternative, not an embellishment

Here a point *denotes a predictive state*. Primitives are \(X\), contexts \(C\),
an outcome measurable space \(\Omega\), kernels
\(K_c:X\to\mathcal P(\Omega)\), evidence events \(e\), and partial updates
\(U_e:X\rightharpoonup X\).

Candidate axioms:

1. **Extensionality.** \(x=y\) iff \(K_c(x)=K_c(y)\) for every \(c\).
2. **Supported closure.** If \(K_c(x)(e)>0\), then \(U_e(x)\) exists.
3. **Conditioning correctness.** For measurable \(A\),
   \(K_c(U_e x)(A)=K_c(x)(A\mid e)\).
4. **Sequential coherence.** Whenever defined,
   \(U_f(U_e x)=U_{e\cap f}(x)\).
5. **Data-processing naturality.** Coarsening outcomes or changing presentation
   commutes with prediction and update.

This gives evidence-update almost for free and does **not** put a Bayesian prior
over coordinates. But it assumes much more: calibrated outcome laws, a chosen
event algebra, and closure under conditioning. Many hidden activations are
causal states without being denotations of normalized distributions. Starting
here would define away much of the object we want to understand.

I would therefore start with A. Its primitive is only observable
substitutability. Probabilities can instantiate its probes later; they are not
ontological furniture.

## 2. First construct: closeness as a filter, not a number

Closeness has the best ratio. It needs neither closure of \(X\) under update nor
a typing rule for composition, yet it immediately disagrees with global cosine
whenever Axiom 6 holds. Evidence-update needs an event algebra; composition
needs interfaces and types; inference is downstream of both.

Define the **closeness profile** of \(y\) relative to anchor \(x\) by

\[
\Phi_x(y)=\{c\in C:y\in N_c(x)\}.
\]

Say \(y\succeq_x z\) when \(\Phi_x(z)\subseteq\Phi_x(y)\): every probe that
accepts \(z\) as a substitute also accepts \(y\). Incomparability is allowed.
This is directed, context-indexed, and ordinal. A scalar distance is a possible
representation theorem to be earned, not a definition.

**Proposition.** Axioms 1–4 induce a \(T_0\) topology on \(X\), with
\(\{N_c(x):c\in C,x\in X\}\) as a neighborhood basis. Axiom 5 makes this
topology invariant under presentation isomorphism.

**Proof sketch.** Self-substitution covers \(X\). If
\(z\in N_c(x)\cap N_d(y)\), local transport supplies \(e,f\) with
\(N_e(z)\subseteq N_c(x)\) and \(N_f(z)\subseteq N_d(y)\); finite conjunction
then gives \(N_{e\otimes f}(z)\) inside the intersection. Thus the basic sets
generate a topology. If distinct \(x,y\) could not be topologically
distinguished, each would lie in every basic neighborhood of the other,
contradicting observational separation. Covariance carries basic neighborhoods
to basic neighborhoods, so each presentation isomorphism is a homeomorphism.

This proposition is deliberately modest. It proves that topology is available
before metric geometry. It does not prove that the topology predicts anything;
the experiment below must earn that.

## 3. First falsifier: held-out substitutability versus global geometry

Use the cached Qwen3-0.6B model in CPU float32 and `eval()` mode. Pin the exact
model revision, tokenizer revision, torch/transformers versions, and whether
input/output embeddings are tied. No generation is needed.

One script should do the following.

1. Hard-code 64 common English lexical items, stratified into 16 nouns, verbs,
   adjectives, and function words. Retain an item only if its leading-space form
   is exactly one non-special token; abort if fewer than 48 survive. Record all
   exclusions.
2. Hard-code 16 neutral carrier probes in four semantic blocks, four paraphrases
   per block: dictionary gloss, likely continuation, topical association, and
   grammatical use. Construct `inputs_embeds` by inserting the candidate token
   vector into exactly the same tokenized prefix/suffix slot, so sequence length
   and every other token are fixed.
3. For each probe \(p\) and state \(x\), record the full next-token law
   \(K_p(x)\). Repeat one batch twice; the maximum absolute log-probability
   difference is the numerical null.
4. Instantiate directed probe loss by
   \(r_p(x\to y)=D_{KL}(K_p(x)\Vert K_p(y))\). For a probe block \(B\), use
   the median \(D_B(x\to y)\) over its four paraphrases. This defines
   threshold neighborhoods \(N_{B,\epsilon}(x)=\{y:D_B(x\to y)<\epsilon\}\).
5. Predeclare two blocks as calibration and two as held-out. For every anchor,
   compare all candidate pairs \(y,z\). Predict which has lower held-out loss
   using (i) the median calibration-block loss and (ii) raw cosine, Euclidean
   distance, centered cosine, coordinate-wise standardized cosine, all-but-top
   cosine after removing 1 and 3 principal components fitted on a fixed 8,192
   vocabulary-row sample, norm difference, and the KL between
   \(\operatorname{softmax}(E_xW_U^\top)\) laws as a one-step unembedding
   baseline (report not-applicable if the dimensions do not compose). Report
   pairwise accuracy and rank correlation; discard held-out ties within ten
   numerical-null units. Bootstrap anchors, not ordered triples.
6. Separately search for Axiom-6 witnesses. A reversal is admissible only when
   one block favors \(y\) over \(z\) in at least three of four paraphrases,
   another favors \(z\) over \(y\) in at least three of four, and both median
   gaps exceed ten times the numerical null. Print the actual words, contexts,
   KL values, and cosine values for every witness.

**Precommitted interpretation.** The native construct wins this first probe only
if calibration neighborhoods beat the *best* imported baseline on held-out
pairwise accuracy by at least 10 percentage points and the anchor-clustered 95%
bootstrap interval for the gain excludes zero. A stable reversal is a concrete
fact that no symmetric, context-free cosine ordering can express.

**Wrong means wrong.** If there is no admissible reversal among all tested
triples, Axiom 6 is false on this measured lexical subspace and probe family. If
the best imported baseline matches or beats calibration neighborhoods on
held-out probes, the claim that this native construct has predictive value here
is killed even if the topology remains formally valid. If only raw cosine loses
but centered, standardized, all-but-top, norm, or unembedding controls recover
the result, the finding is an ordinary coordinate confound, not native
mathematics.

The simplest global confound is token frequency/norm leaking through tied
embeddings; the baseline panel is designed to test it before interpreting any
row. The other obvious confound is a bad carrier family; held-out semantic
blocks and paraphrase agreement test that.

## 4. Prior art: boundaries, not a novelty claim

- **Embedding geometry.** Word2vec-style analogy and later geometric probing
  show that useful relations can be extracted from coordinates. Ordinal and
  neighborhood methods already show that comparisons can precede distances.
  They usually ask which Euclidean/manifold structure the learned points have;
  we ask which structure survives replacement of the presentation while
  preserving probes.
- **Anisotropy and hubness.** Ethayarajh's
  [contextual-embedding study](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1006/) and subsequent
  local-isotropy work show that raw cosine is strongly distribution- and
  layer-dependent. This motivates the rejection but does not supply a semantic
  replacement: centering or deleting dominant components repairs a chosen
  coordinate metric.
- **Hyperbolic/Poincaré embeddings.** Nickel and Kiela's
  [Poincaré embeddings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/7213-poincare-embeddings-for-learning-hierarchical-representations)
  answer how to represent hierarchies parsimoniously once negative curvature
  and a metric are chosen. That is excellent model geometry, not an axiom-free
  account of why a semantic probe should respect that metric.
- **Categorical/compositional semantics.** Coecke, Sadrzadeh, and Clark
  [derive sentence composition from grammatical morphisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4394).
  The compositional discipline is native in spirit. Concrete DisCoCat models,
  however, commonly land in finite-dimensional vector spaces and tensor/inner-
  product structure. Coalgebraic behavioral equivalence is an even closer
  ancestor of our observational quotient; we should claim inheritance, not
  invention.
- **Information geometry.** Fisher–Rao geometry answers how a manifold of
  probability laws should be measured invariantly, and natural gradient uses
  that answer. It is native when a point already is a probability law. A hidden
  state does not automatically denote one; choosing a decoder kernel is extra
  structure. Axiomatization B is the honest entry point for this lineage.
- **Sheaves and topology.** Persistent homology extracts global shape from local
  neighborhoods; sheaves formalize compatible local data and obstructions to a
  global section. Abramsky and Brandenburger's
  [sheaf-theoretic contextuality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264) is especially
  relevant to non-collapse. But ML topology is often induced from Euclidean
  distances, while our neighborhoods are generated by probes. Sheaf language
  becomes useful only after we have overlapping contexts and an actual gluing
  question.
- **Metric learning theory.** The
  [metric-learning literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6709) learns global,
  local, nonlinear, and structured similarities from task supervision and gives
  generalization analyses. It already answers “which scalar comparison helps
  this task?” It does not justify symmetry, triangle inequalities, scalarization,
  or task-independent identity; our first experiment must beat strong learned
  or repaired metric baselines before claiming a real difference.

## 6. What Claude should attack hardest

1. **Probe circularity.** Have I defined meaning as “whatever this decoder
   currently does,” thereby canonizing model bugs and making operational
   prediction tautological? What independent criterion separates semantic probes
   from arbitrary readouts?
2. **Axiom versus empirical richness.** Should contextual non-collapse be an
   axiom, a theorem target, or merely a classifier of latent spaces? Am I rigging
   the ontology to reject a genuinely metric latent space in advance?
3. **Mathematical content.** Is the topology proposition only the standard
   neighborhood-basis theorem with renamed objects? Demand the first result that
   constrains a measurable latent phenomenon or rules out an imported operation;
   if this axiom set cannot yield one, replace it.
