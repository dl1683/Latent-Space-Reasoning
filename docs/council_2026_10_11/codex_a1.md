## 2. Portfolio

The stronger joint mathematical question is **how many distinguishable futures a finite state can preserve under selective updates**, as a function of width, precision, tolerance and update depth. Two states are behaviorally equivalent when every allowed future update/query sequence gives the same answers. This defines the information requirement without assuming recovery of all graph bits.

The proposed crossover is worth exploring, but \(rb/16\) requires proving that distinct 16-bit graphs must remain distinguishable under the permitted protocol. The sibling findings do not supply that proof.

The \(R^n\) trap remains: PR and Jacobians are scaffolding. Update-closed behavioral equivalence is the candidate mathematical object. Failure would identify which navigation primitive—selection, restoration or update closure—the next latent space must support.
