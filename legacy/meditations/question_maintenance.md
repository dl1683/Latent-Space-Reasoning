# Question Maintenance?

## What Is Being Maintained?
- What exactly is the object of maintenance when the system says it is preserving reasoning rather than preserving files, prompts, checkpoints, metrics, or social confidence?
- What pattern must remain continuous for the project to still be the same project after its code, documents, benchmark results, and explanatory frames have all been rewritten?
- What information would have to survive total implementation replacement for the reasoning paradigm to remain intact?
- What should be treated as the maintained identity of the system: its outputs, its process, its question hierarchy, its evidence discipline, its failure memory, its selection pressure, or its capacity to revise itself?
- What are the smallest invariants that must remain legible across time for future work to be continuation rather than reinvention?
- Which parts of the repo are organs that must keep functioning, which are scaffolds that can be removed, and which are dead tissue that only looks like memory?
- What would a maintainer need to inspect to know whether the system's reasoning capability is healthier than it was yesterday?
- What would count as a loss of reasoning identity even if all tests still passed?

## What Decays Without Maintenance?
- What degrades first in a reasoning system: factual accuracy, causal contact, calibration, question selection, taste, source discipline, metric relevance, or the will to notice contradictions?
- Which artifacts become dangerous not because they are wrong at creation but because their authority outlives their freshness?
- What kinds of documentation rot into traps because they preserve old confidence without preserving the conditions under which that confidence was earned?
- What errors compound when each locally rational patch adds a special case that future agents mistake for architecture?
- What does software rot look like when the software is not only code but also beliefs, benchmarks, prompts, rituals, and interpretations?
- What would be the equivalent of protein misfolding in this repo: a concept that still exists, still links to other concepts, but now causes every downstream question to fold incorrectly?
- What is the earliest visible symptom that the maintenance workload has exceeded the system's capacity to repair itself?
- What stale beliefs are still metabolically expensive because they force every new question to route around them?

## What Is The Maintenance Budget?
- How much attention, compute, review, deletion, consolidation, and remeasurement must be spent merely to keep reasoning from getting worse?
- What is the basal metabolic rate of this project if no new capabilities are added and the only goal is to preserve truthfulness, coherence, and usability?
- What recurring work is invisible because it prevents failures rather than producing visible features?
- Which maintenance tasks are being mistaken for optional cleanup even though they are actually the cost of keeping the paradigm alive?
- How should the system decide between spending scarce effort on new search versus repairing old search, new artifacts versus consolidating old artifacts, and new claims versus retiring old claims?
- What is the entropy accumulation rate of the repo, and what evidence would show that maintenance investment is below it?
- Which parts of maintenance should be automatic, which require human judgment, and which should require adversarial review before they can change anything?
- What is the smallest maintenance loop that would prevent the project from quietly becoming unreadable to its next serious user?

## What Is Staleness?
- When does a question become stale: when its answer changes, when its assumptions change, when its evidence base changes, when its priority changes, or when it stops producing better questions?
- How can the system distinguish a question that is old but still central from a question that is old because it has been avoided?
- What timestamp is actually meaningful for a belief: creation time, last source verification, last failed prediction, last successful use, last adversarial challenge, or last downstream dependency?
- What would make a meditation question stale even if it still sounds profound?
- Which questions should decay unless refreshed by new evidence, and which questions should persist because they define the frame in which evidence is interpreted?
- How should the system ask whether a file is stale without treating recency as a proxy for truth?
- What would a staleness audit need to ask about every claim, every benchmark, every roadmap, and every inherited category?
- What forms of staleness are hidden by polish, completeness, length, citations, or confident organization?

## What Is Drift?
- What is the difference between capability drift, value drift, metric drift, language drift, benchmark drift, user-intent drift, and question-selection drift?
- Which drift matters most if the system's goal is not simply to perform tasks but to change the paradigm of reasoning?
- How can the project detect that its vocabulary has kept the same words while the underlying meanings have moved?
- What would indicate that the repo is optimizing for artifacts that look like reasoning rather than mechanisms that actually improve reasoning?
- Which metrics are at risk of becoming ceremonies once the system learns how to satisfy them without preserving the underlying cognitive property?
- What drift would remain invisible if the system only compared current outputs to old outputs instead of comparing current questions to old failure modes?
- How can the project tell the difference between healthy adaptation and slow abandonment of its central thesis?
- What would be the equivalent of alignment drift in a persistent research repo?

## What Is Repair?
- What does it mean to repair a question rather than answer it?
- When a question fails, should the system narrow it, split it, contextualize it, delete it, demote it, test it, or ask what kind of failure it is?
- What repair action corresponds to each diagnosis: missing evidence, wrong category, stale source, vague mechanism, untested causal claim, false analogy, overloaded word, or bad selection pressure?
- How can repair preserve useful scar tissue without preserving accidental damage?
- What would make a patch to the reasoning corpus high fidelity rather than merely locally plausible?
- What should be repaired by adding redundancy, what should be repaired by pruning, and what should be repaired by changing the question's environment?
- How can the system avoid repairs that reduce visible failure while increasing hidden coupling?
- What would a repair log need to preserve so future agents can understand why the previous shape was changed?

## What Is Repair Fidelity?
- How can the project estimate whether each correction introduces smaller errors than the errors it removes?
- What is the reasoning equivalent of shifting from high-fidelity repair to error-prone repair as maintenance pressure increases?
- When do fast fixes become mutational load on the project?
- Which emergency patches should expire unless later rebuilt under calmer conditions?
- How can a future agent tell whether a cleanup simplified the project or deleted necessary context?
- What checks would reveal that repair has become performative because it changes surface organization without improving causal contact?
- What would it mean for a question-maintenance process to have a measurable error rate?
- What should happen when the repair mechanism itself becomes a source of degradation?

## What Is Error Detection?
- What sensors does the system have for detecting degradation in reasoning before degradation appears as failed tasks?
- What anomaly should trigger suspicion: increased verbosity, repeated categories, missing falsifiers, old citations, unclear ownership, inconsistent naming, benchmark optimism, or inability to identify what changed?
- How can the system detect that an artifact is overfit to a prior conversation rather than reusable by a future agent?
- What would count as a prediction error for the question corpus itself?
- Which errors can be detected by syntax, which by tests, which by benchmark deltas, which by source comparison, and which only by human unease?
- How should the system detect contradictions between meditations, docs, code, and benchmark evidence without collapsing all nuance into a single canonical answer?
- What is the syndrome measurement for a reasoning system: what can be observed about corruption without destroying or overwriting the information being protected?
- What failures should be deliberately injected so the system can learn whether its repair loops work?

## What Is Error Correction?
- What redundant information lets the project reconstruct a damaged belief without trusting any single artifact?
- Which sources should be treated as the template strand when a mismatch appears between old memory and new evidence?
- What should happen when two apparently authoritative artifacts disagree about the project direction?
- What does majority vote fail to correct when most artifacts inherited the same earlier mistake?
- When should correction mean restoring the prior pattern, and when should correction mean updating the pattern because the world changed?
- How can the system verify that correction did not erase minority evidence that was actually the early signal?
- What does layered correction look like across syntax, tests, benchmark scoring, source provenance, conceptual review, and user intent?
- What would make the system's error correction adaptive rather than merely scripted?

## What Is Forgetting?
- What should this project deliberately forget so that retrieval becomes sharper rather than noisier?
- Which artifacts are retained because they are useful, and which are retained because deletion feels like disrespecting effort?
- What is the difference between forgetting a detail, forgetting a mistake, forgetting an invariant, and forgetting why an invariant mattered?
- How can active forgetting improve generalization without creating amnesia about failure?
- Which old questions should become less accessible unless refreshed by new use, and which should remain highly accessible because they protect against recurring failure?
- What would retrieval-induced forgetting look like in a repo where repeatedly using one frame suppresses nearby alternative frames?
- Which concepts should decay by default unless they continue to earn relevance through prediction, implementation, or adversarial review?
- What deletion would make the project more intelligent rather than merely cleaner?

## What Is Renewal?
- When does maintenance need to preserve a pattern, and when does it need to regenerate the pattern from deeper rules?
- What would count as healthy renewal rather than cosmetic rebranding?
- Which parts of the system must periodically die so that the reasoning organism does not become trapped in its own old scaffolding?
- What is the project equivalent of apoptosis: a local artifact removing itself to prevent systemic corruption?
- How can the repo renew its question frontier without losing the lineage that explains why the frontier moved?
- What reset rituals would restore alignment between mission, evidence, metrics, and current implementation?
- When should renewal be gradual replacement like Ship of Theseus maintenance, and when should it be a discontinuous refounding?
- What would a future user experience as renewal rather than churn?

## What Is Graceful Degradation?
- If the system cannot preserve everything, what should fail first?
- What minimum reasoning capability must remain when context, compute, time, documentation, or benchmark coverage is limited?
- Which parts of the meditation corpus should collapse into summaries under pressure, and which should remain expanded because compression would destroy the useful structure?
- What would graceful degradation look like for question generation: fewer questions, narrower questions, more grounded questions, more uncertainty, or more explicit handoff?
- How can the project degrade in a way that exposes its limits instead of hiding them?
- What would a low-resource future agent need to know to continue the central reasoning work without reopening every artifact?
- Which redundancies are worth keeping because they let the system lose one path without losing the pattern?
- What degradation mode would be catastrophic even if the repo still looked organized?

## What Is Redundancy?
- What should be duplicated because it protects against corruption, and what should not be duplicated because it creates inconsistent shadows?
- How can the system distinguish useful redundancy from documentation sprawl?
- Which ideas need multiple encodings: code, tests, readme, benchmark, meditation, failure log, and source map?
- What redundancy lets a future agent triangulate truth when one source is stale?
- Where should there be independent checks rather than copied summaries?
- What is the difference between backup, replication, degeneracy, and true alternative strategy in this reasoning system?
- Which redundant paths should be deliberately different so they can compensate for different failure modes?
- What would be the redundancy budget for a project that wants to be usable rather than overwhelming?

## What Maintains The Maintainer?
- What prevents the maintenance process from becoming stale, overconfident, bureaucratic, or adversarial to real discovery?
- Who or what audits the audits?
- What signals show that cleanup discipline has started optimizing for neatness instead of truth?
- How can the system preserve enough mess for exploration while preventing mess from becoming entropy?
- What incentives make a maintainer hide decay, and what incentives make decay easy to surface early?
- What should force a maintainer to ask deeper questions before making visible changes?
- How can the user, future agents, tests, benchmarks, and source documents form a maintenance ecology rather than a chain of isolated interventions?
- What would meta-maintenance require beyond recurring cleanup: monitoring the repair rules, updating the staleness model, revising deletion criteria, and testing whether tests still test the right thing?

## What Maintenance Should The Next Question Require?
- Before asking the next question, what existing question does it refresh, challenge, split, retire, or make more precise?
- What evidence would the next question need to remain alive beyond this meditation?
- What hidden maintenance cost does the next question impose on the corpus?
- How will the next question avoid becoming another artifact that future agents must interpret without knowing why it exists?
- What older question becomes newly important if this question is asked?
- What new failure mode becomes visible only after this question is added?
- What should be deleted, demoted, or archived if this question proves more central than its ancestors?
- What deeper question is needed to maintain the ability to keep asking maintainable questions?
