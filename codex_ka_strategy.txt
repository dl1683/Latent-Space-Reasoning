Bottom line: \(K_A\) is the strongest current research lead, but \(L\!\to\!C\approx0.72\) is still a post-hoc, surface-specific fitted regularity—not yet a native stable equation. I would pivot the program toward validating it, while keeping the public claim explicitly provisional.

## 1. Stable equation or fitted statistic?

It is an unusually promising equation candidate, for three reasons:

- The coefficient is stable across d2–d4.
- A d2+d3 fit predicts d4 well: \(r=0.960\), mean TV \(\approx0.019\) with the shipped estimator.
- It compresses the depth-scaling pattern: deeper instances contain more shadow mass, so a roughly fixed correction map produces a larger absolute gain.

But four qualifications prevent calling it a stable law yet.

First, the quoted matrices correspond specifically to the literal suffix `# No side effects.\n` in the [Gate1b raw configuration](<C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/Latent-Space-Reasoning/experiments/results/svb_qwen3_gate1b/result.json:42>). They are not an ASSERT-class mean, and Gate 2 uses a different assertion surface, `# {var} is unchanged.\n`.

Second, other ASSERT surfaces have similarly stable but very different coefficients:

- Across individual surfaces, depth variation is usually small.
- Across surfaces, \(L\!\to\!C\) ranges from approximately \(0.475\) to \(0.861\).

The stronger emerging hypothesis is therefore a surface-indexed family \(K_s\), not one universal \(K_A\).

Third, only the well-supported part of the matrix is stable. Between d2 and d4:

- The fitted \(L\) column changes little.
- The \(R\) column has TV \(\approx0.466\).

The input clouds are also ill-conditioned, especially at d2, so the complete \(3\times3\) matrix is not identified nearly as securely as the \(L\)-correction component.

Fourth, the exact value depends on fitting procedure. A directly constrained stochastic fit gives \(L\!\to\!C\approx0.746,0.739,0.744\), rather than \(0.734,0.715,0.720\). The invariant appears real; “0.72” is not yet a canonical constant.

One wording correction matters: “converts 72% of shadow probability” is too literal. In the fitted matrices, \(L\!\to\!R\) is nonzero, and the observed cellwise correct-gain divided by baseline shadow mass is about \(0.854,0.853,0.866\). The matrix coefficient is an inferred pure-\(L\) column, extrapolated from the observed response manifold—not direct tracking of probability parcels.

My classification is:

> **Post-hoc depth-stable response-map candidate**, one rung below a stable equation.

## 2. What would make it native?

The stochastic matrix itself is ordinary \(\mathbb R^3\)/Markov machinery. That is legitimate scaffolding, but it is not the native object.

The native claim would be that the suffix induces a well-defined action on a response-defined quotient. Let \(r(z)\) denote the behavior available to a latent-space denizen, and define

\[
z\sim_r z' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r(z)=r(z').
\]

The suffix \(s\) descends to that quotient only if

\[
z\sim_r z'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_s z\sim_r T_s z'.
\]

Only then is

\[
[z]\mapsto[T_s z]
\]

an intrinsic operation. A matrix equation

\[
r(T_s z)\approx K_s r(z)
\]

would then be a coordinate representation of that native operation.

The present \(R^2=0.79\!-\!0.98\) does not establish this. The [lumpability analyzer](<C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/Latent-Space-Reasoning/experiments/analyze_lumpability.py:61>) averages surfaces within roles and fits an in-sample scalar regression of post-\(L\) on baseline-\(L\). It does not test response-matched collisions, the complete output law, or states reached through other transformations. The theory document already states the stronger condition in its [lumpability section](<C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/Latent-Space-Reasoning/theory/INTENSIONAL_DESCENT.md:342>).

Graduation therefore requires:

1. **Closure:** response-equivalent states remain response-equivalent after the suffix.
2. **Held-out prediction:** one frozen \(K_s\) predicts complete response laws for unseen state families.
3. **Off-manifold action:** it works on states reached through previously unseen predecessor operations, not merely ordinary depth variation.
4. **Predictive surplus:** it beats logit-shift, monotone recalibration, mean-displacement, and one-parameter correction baselines.
5. **Representative descent:** if claiming an ASSERT-class operator, the same law must transfer to wholly held-out assertion surfaces.
6. **Composition:** eventually,
   \[
   r(T_sT_u z)\approx K_s\,r(T_u z)
   \]
   for unseen \(u\), without refitting.

If collision closure passes but the matrix fails, that would still be valuable: it would mean a native quotient action exists, but its law is nonlinear or requires a richer response state.

## 3. Should it replace noncommutativity as the headline?

As the **research focus: yes**. As a **confirmed headline: not yet**.

A defensible current sentence is:

> On the fixed suffix `# No side effects.`, a three-bin response map fitted on depths 2–3 predicts depth-4 changes with gain correlation \(r=0.96\) and mean TV approximately \(0.019\); this post-hoc result nominates a depth-stable, surface-specific correction law for prospective testing.

I would not currently say:

- “ASSERT has a universal 72% correction operator.”
- “The suffix transports 72% of shadow mass.”
- “A native algebraic law has been discovered.”

The fact that the cross-depth map ties your strongest naive baseline on TV is decisive here: high correlation may mostly reflect both models recognizing that gain grows with available shadow mass. In my reconstruction, one-parameter transfer and constant-logit-shift baselines reach TV around \(0.0215\) and \(0.0220\), only modestly worse than the full projected \(K_s\). That is suggestive, not a decisive predictive surplus.

The strongest remaining rival is:

> **A fixed lexical suffix induces a surface-specific logit/readout recalibration along the narrow natural response manifold.** Depth merely changes where examples lie on that manifold, making the same recalibration look like a stable probability-transport operator.

That explanation accounts for coefficient stability and depth-scaled gains without requiring latent probability transport, lumpability, or native algebra.

The composition result does not independently validate this operator: Gate 2 uses a different assertion surface, and its noncommutativity remains provisional.

## 4. The decisive held-out test

The strongest test is a **prospectively frozen response-collision and off-manifold transfer test**, not another depth correlation.

Freeze one literal suffix \(s\), its response coordinates, fitting method, thresholds, and baselines. Fit \(K_s\) only on designated d2–d3 calibration families. Then evaluate on:

- Unseen templates, names, and values.
- New depths.
- Completely held-out predecessor suffix families \(u\).
- Pairs of histories \(z,z'\) deliberately selected to have nearly identical pre-suffix response laws despite different latent histories.

Test two separate claims:

\[
d(r(z),r(z'))\leq\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d(r(T_s z),r(T_s z'))\leq\epsilon
\]

for quotient closure, and

\[
d\!\left(r(T_sT_u z),K_s\,r(T_u z)\right)\leq\epsilon
\]

for operator prediction.

The result graduates only if clustered confidence bounds show both:

- Absolute held-out error below a preregistered tolerance.
- A positive margin over the best frozen rival:
  \[
  \operatorname{err}(K_s)+m
  <
  \min_b \operatorname{err}(b),
  \]
  where \(b\) includes constant logit shift, monotone recalibration, mean displacement, identity, and the one-parameter \(C\!\leftrightarrow\!L\) model.

Why this is decisive:

- Failure of response-matched collisions means \(K_s\) is merely a projection-dependent summary.
- Passing collisions but failing matrix prediction means a native action may exist, but \(C,L,R\) are insufficient coordinates.
- Passing both, including unseen predecessor states and baseline surplus, would justify calling \(K_s\) a stable quotient law.
- Transfer across held-out surface representatives would then justify promoting \(K_s\) to a class operator \(K_A\).

So the next move should be one bounded, preregistered CPU experiment aimed directly at quotient closure and predictive surplus. That is currently higher leverage than further composition experiments.
